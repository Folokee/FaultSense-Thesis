\chapter{Related Work}
\label{ch:related-work}

\section{Introduction}

The field of fault detection and diagnosis in photovoltaic systems has attracted growing research interest over the past decade, driven by the rapid expansion of solar installations and the corresponding need for reliable automated monitoring solutions. This chapter reviews the existing literature with the objective of situating the contributions of this work within the current state of the art. The review is organized around three distinct families of diagnostic approaches, differentiated by the type of input data they exploit: thermographic imaging, current-voltage characteristic curve analysis, and operational electrical and meteorological measurements. Before examining these methodological families, it is necessary to characterize the datasets available in the field, as the nature of the training data fundamentally conditions the validity of performance claims and the generalizability of the proposed models. The chapter closes with a critical synthesis of the identified limitations and a statement of the research gaps that motivate the contributions of this thesis.

\section{Data Survey}
\label{sec:data-survey}
% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The availability of labeled, high-quality data is the foundational prerequisite for any supervised or semi-supervised learning approach to PV fault diagnosis. A comprehensive survey of open datasets for assessing PV system reliability highlights a striking asymmetry in the current data landscape: fault-labeled datasets collected from real field installations are rare, primarily because acquiring them requires either waiting for faults to occur naturally, a process that may span years, or organizing dedicated measurement campaigns in which fault conditions are artificially induced under controlled conditions \citep{chen2025datasets}. Simulated datasets, by contrast, can be readily reproduced from circuit models using tools such as MATLAB/Simulink and are consequently far more abundant in the literature, though their idealized statistical properties limit their relevance to real operational scenarios. The picture that emerges from this survey is one of significant data scarcity. The number of publicly available, fault-labeled datasets based on genuine field measurements remains extremely limited, and those that exist cover a narrow range of climatic conditions, PV technologies, and fault types. This scarcity has pushed a substantial fraction of the research community toward circuit-simulated data, typically produced by injecting fault conditions into MATLAB/Simulink PV models, which introduces a fundamental validity concern: models trained on idealized circuit responses may fail to generalize when confronted with the sensor imperfections, thermal drift, environmental variability, and installation-specific behavior inherent to real field measurements. A further obstacle is the absence of any cross-dataset standardization: the available labeled datasets differ in their measurement channels, sampling frequencies, and fault taxonomies, making direct aggregation or multi-source fusion impractical.

\section{Thermal Image-Based Methods}
\label{sec:thermal}
% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Infrared thermography has been widely adopted as a diagnostic modality in PV fault detection research, primarily for its ability to reveal hotspot anomalies invisible to conventional optical inspection. In a healthy module, temperature is approximately uniform across the cell surface under steady irradiance. Faults such as shading, cell cracking, bypass diode failures, and soiling create localised thermal dissipation patterns detectable by infrared cameras. The typical pipeline acquires aerial images via UAVs equipped with thermal sensors, applies preprocessing to normalise illumination and correct geometric distortions, then feeds the result into a classification model.

\paragraph{}
Convolutional neural networks have become the dominant model family for thermographic fault diagnosis. Early work framed the task as binary discrimination: \citet{manno2021thermal} applied convolutional feature extraction to thermographic images from a real installation and achieved classification accuracy exceeding 95\%. Transfer learning was subsequently used to compensate for the scarcity of large labeled infrared datasets: \citet{akram2020transfer} pretrained a lightweight CNN on electroluminescence images and transferred its representation to infrared thermography for module defect detection, reaching 99.23\% accuracy.

The release of the InfraredSolarModules benchmark (20{,}000 cropped module-level thermographs annotated across eleven anomaly categories) enabled the move to fine-grained multi-class diagnosis. \citet{korkmaz2022multiscale} built a three-branch multi-scale CNN on an AlexNet backbone and reported 97.32\% on the binary task and 93.51\% across the eleven anomaly classes.

\paragraph{}
More recent work has shifted from patch classification toward object detection on full UAV frames. \citet{xie2024styolo} extended YOLOv8 with a spatial-channel reconstruction convolution module and triplet attention, improving mean Average Precision while reducing model size by 15\% relative to the YOLOv8s baseline. \citet{dhimish2025hotspot} introduced HOTSPOT-YOLO, a lightweight attention-driven architecture designed for the small, low-contrast thermal anomalies typical of drone-based inspections, reporting a mean Average Precision of 90.8\%.

\subsection{Limitations}
Thermographic approaches carry two fundamental limitations that restrict large-scale operational deployment. First, the acquisition cost is prohibitive for continuous monitoring: calibrated infrared cameras combined with UAV operations place the all-in cost of an inspection campaign in the range of 0.5 to 3.0 euros per module \citep{herz2021}, representing tens to hundreds of thousands of euros for a utility-scale plant. Second, the temporal resolution is incompatible with near real-time fault awareness: surveys are inherently event-driven and infrequent, so a fault that develops between campaigns may propagate undetected for weeks or months. These constraints position thermography as a periodic audit tool rather than a continuous monitoring modality.

\section{Current-Voltage Curve-Based Methods}
\label{sec:ivcurve}
% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The current-voltage (I-V) characteristic curve of a PV module encodes its complete electrical behaviour at a given irradiance and temperature. Under healthy conditions, the curve follows the single-diode model: a quasi-constant current plateau at low voltages, a knee near the maximum power point, and a steep decline to zero at open-circuit voltage. Different faults produce characteristic distortions of this shape (partial shading introduces step-like discontinuities as bypass diodes activate, increased series resistance tilts the knee region, and decreased shunt resistance raises the plateau slope) that serve as discriminative signatures and have motivated a dedicated family of diagnostic approaches.

\paragraph{}
Early methods extract interpretable scalar features from the I-V curve (short-circuit current, open-circuit voltage, fill factor, maximum power point coordinates) and feed them into classical classifiers. \citet{chen2017} trained a kernel extreme learning machine on such features and reported fault diagnosis accuracy above 98\% on a simulated dataset. \citet{eskandari2020} applied ensemble learning to I-V and P-V curve features for line-to-line fault detection, achieving 99\% classification accuracy.

\paragraph{}
More recent work bypasses manual feature extraction entirely. \citet{liu2021sae} used a stacked autoencoder to learn a latent representation of the I-V curve directly, then applied clustering to group measurements into fault categories without labeled data. \citet{qu2024dust} transformed I-V curves into graphical image representations and applied CNNs with convolutional block attention modules (CBAM) under varying soiling and dust conditions, achieving above 99\% on both simulated and experimental data. \citet{gong2024deep} constructed a three-dimensional feature map from current, voltage, and power signals simultaneously; the resulting MIFNet, optimised with an adaptive sparrow search algorithm, reached 99.64\% average recognition accuracy across single- and multi-component fault scenarios.

\subsection{Limitations}
The central limitation of this approachis the nature of the measurement itself: acquiring an I-V curve requires actively sweeping the module or string operating point, which is a discrete diagnostic event rather than a continuous observation. Traditional sweep procedures rely on dedicated tracer instruments and interrupt power generation; more recent inverter-integrated solutions have reduced this overhead but the procedure remains an event-driven sweep dependent on vendor-specific hardware. The accuracy of any sweep is also sensitive to irradiance and temperature changes during acquisition, which can mask subtle fault signatures under variable cloud cover. These constraints position I-V curve analysis as a periodic procedure and motivate the search for methods that operate on continuously available operational measurements.

\section{Electrical and Meteorological Data-Based Methods}
\label{sec:elec-meteo}
% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The third and most practically relevant family of data-driven PV fault detection methods operates on the operational electrical and meteorological measurements that modern PV installations continuously generate as part of their standard monitoring infrastructure. These measurements, including DC voltage and current at the string level, AC power at the inverter output, solar irradiance, and module and ambient temperatures, are already collected by dataloggers and SCADA systems in most grid-connected PV plants without any additional sensing hardware. This key advantage makes electrical and meteorological data-based approaches the most scalable option for continuous, near real-time fault monitoring.

\subsection{Machine Learning Approaches}

Classical machine learning methods were among the first applied to PV fault detection on operational electrical data, and they remain competitive with deep learning on tabular, low-frequency measurements.

\paragraph{}
\citet{suliman2024ml} benchmarked multiple SVM kernel configurations against a Binary Adaptive XGBoost on line-to-line, open-circuit, shading, and MPPT failures, with the boosted tree reaching 87.56\% accuracy. \citet{amiri2024rf} extracted single-diode model parameters via a modified grey wolf optimizer and used them to generate labeled training data for two random forest classifiers (one for binary detection, one for multi-class diagnosis) reaching 99.4\% on both tasks and outperforming SVM, KNN, MLP, decision tree, and SGD baselines. Signal processing front-ends have also been paired with classical classifiers: \citet{cai2022arc} applied variational mode decomposition to decompose string current signals into intrinsic mode functions before classifying them with a PSO-tuned SVM, enabling detection of arc fault signatures invisible in the raw time domain; \citet{saiprakash_improved_2024} extracted voltage and current features through the Stockwell transform and fed them to data mining classifiers, reporting 99.61\% accuracy on partial shading, open-circuit, and degradation faults.

\paragraph{}
Gradient boosting algorithms have shown strong performance on tabular PV fault data. \citet{noura_explainable_2025} built a two-phase Extra Trees framework achieving 99.5\% detection and 98.7\% diagnosis accuracy, with integrated SHAP and LIME explainability. \citet{kull_adaptive_2025} paired a Deep Belief Network for feature extraction with LightGBM for classification on a 100~MW SCADA dataset, reaching 98.21\% on a seven-class problem. \citet{zwirtes_fault_2025} specifically studied generalization: classifiers trained on one real PV installation showed significant performance degradation when applied to another with different module characteristics, highlighting the installation-specific nature of fault signatures. For settings where fault labels are unavailable, \citet{kabir_isolation_2023} demonstrated Isolation Forest on PV operational data, while \citet{qu2022unsupervised} combined unsupervised weather pattern recognition with blending fitting models for label-free fault detection.

\subsection{Deep Learning Approaches}

Deep learning enables automatic extraction of temporal and structural features from raw electrical measurements, reducing dependence on manual feature engineering.

\paragraph{}
Early neural network architectures established the feasibility of learned fault representations for PV systems. \citet{garoudja2017ml} proposed a probabilistic neural network for fault detection and diagnosis on the DC side of a grid-connected system, outperforming a conventional ANN under both clean and noisy conditions. Fully connected multilayer perceptrons have similarly been applied to structured electrical measurements: \citet{vieira2022mlp} compared MLP and probabilistic neural networks for identifying short-circuited modules and disconnected strings, with the MLP achieving approximately 97\% accuracy and demonstrating improved noise robustness over alternative classifiers.

\paragraph{}
Recurrent architectures address the sequential nature of PV operational data. \citet{hajji2023irradiance} introduced a Bidirectional LSTM framework trained on voltage and current measurements under varying irradiance conditions, classifying line-to-line, line-to-ground, bypass diode, and connectivity faults with diagnostic accuracy approaching 100\% on simulated datasets. \citet{amiri2024dl} extended this direction by combining one-dimensional convolutional feature extraction with a bidirectional gated recurrent unit, showing that the joint exploitation of spatial and sequential representations outperforms either modality alone at near-perfect accuracy.

\paragraph{}
Autoencoder-based designs serve both as supervised feature extractors and as unsupervised anomaly detectors: \citet{seghiour2023autoencoder} compressed operating data with a deep autoencoder and fed its encoded representation to a feed-forward classifier for multi-class fault diagnosis, reaching 99.9\% on a 9.54~kW grid-connected installation. \citet{titouni_hybrid_2025} evaluated a hybrid CNN-Autoencoder on the GPVS-Faults dataset, achieving 98.84\% test accuracy. \citet{attouri_adaptive_2026} combined a polynomial Kolmogorov-Arnold network with autoencoder-based dimensionality reduction; on the photovoltaic dataset the model reached 96.47\% accuracy while reducing execution time by over 88\% and memory usage by 40\%.

\paragraph{}
\citet{yan_som-dbtgnet_2025} introduced SOM-DBTGNet, pairing self-organizing maps for anomaly scoring with a dual-branch temporal graph convolution network that explicitly models topological relationships between measurement channels, achieving 90.7\% accuracy across six fault categories. Transformer-based architectures have also been explored for PV fault classification: \citet{khalil2024transformer} leveraged attention mechanisms to analyse the rate of change of PV electrical parameters, achieving 95.8\% classification accuracy while outperforming traditional machine learning baselines on fault forecasting. More recently, \citet{balachandran2025solarfaultnet} proposed a Swin Transformer architecture integrating hierarchical shifted-window attention with probabilistic generative modeling, reporting precision values exceeding 99\% on PV array, inverter, and grid synchronization faults.

Three patterns emerge from this synthesis. First, reported accuracies are consistently high across all modalities and method families, frequently in the 90 to 99\% band and occasionally approaching 100\%; these figures must nevertheless be interpreted with caution because a substantial fraction of the reviewed studies rely on circuit-level simulation rather than field measurements, or evaluate on small or artificially class-balanced test sets. Second, the electrical and meteorological modality is the most extensively represented in the literature, consistent with the observation that operational electrical measurements are the most readily available data type in real PV installations. Third, although several recent contributions explicitly pursue computationally efficient designs, ranging from the model-size reduction of ST-YOLO \citep{xie2024styolo} to the lightweight attention architecture of HOTSPOT-YOLO \citep{dhimish2025hotspot} and the 88\% execution-time and 40\% memory reduction reported for the PolyKAN-AE pipeline \citep{attouri_adaptive_2026}, efficiency in the reviewed literature is most often expressed through proxy metrics computed on workstation-class hardware, such as parameter count, floating-point operations, or theoretical inference time. End-to-end deployment on a representative embedded platform, with measured inference latency, memory footprint, and power consumption obtained on the target device under realistic operational conditions, has been reported only by a small number of works, notably the embedded monitoring system of \citet{costa2020sensors}.

\section{Research Gaps and Motivation}
\label{sec:gap}
% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The preceding review, combined with the dataset landscape analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:data-survey}, identifies several interconnected gaps that motivate the contributions of this thesis.

\textbf{The dominance of simulation-based validation.} A substantial fraction of published methods is trained and evaluated exclusively on circuit-level simulated data, which does not reproduce the sensor noise, missing samples, and weather-driven nonlinearity of real operational recordings. The scarcity of publicly available, fault-labeled field datasets limits the empirical basis on which real-world diagnostic capability can be assessed.

\textbf{Low-signal faults and detection boundary ambiguity.} Certain fault conditions produce only low-amplitude deviations that overlap with normal operating variability, making the decision boundary inherently ambiguous. Reconstruction-based anomaly detectors are particularly exposed: a model may reconstruct low-signal faults with sufficiently low error to suppress the anomaly score below any practical threshold. Class imbalance in real field data further sharpens this difficulty by limiting the calibration of the decision surface in the ambiguous region.

\textbf{Measured embedded deployment under operational conditions.} A growing number of contributions target lightweight designs, yet on-device validation under realistic conditions has been reported by only a small number of works \citep{costa2020sensors}. Most efficiency claims remain expressed through proxy metrics computed on workstation-class hardware rather than measurements obtained on the target device.

\textbf{Distributional shift between training and operational conditions.} PV system behaviour evolves continuously with module ageing, seasonal soiling, and weather regime changes, creating a progressive divergence between the training distribution and the one encountered at deployment. This is aggravated by the short measurement campaigns from which fault-labelled data is typically obtained, which span weeks rather than a full annual cycle, so that a model never observes the seasonal range of operating conditions it will later face. Yet this drift is rarely monitored or accounted for in the design of the proposed diagnostic systems.

\section*{Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Conclusion}

Part~I has established the theoretical foundations of this work. Chapter~\ref{ch:pv-systems-fdd} introduced the operating principles of photovoltaic systems and the taxonomy of faults that affect them, while Section~\ref{sec:ai-fdd} reviewed the machine learning and deep learning techniques relevant to fault detection and diagnosis. The present chapter surveyed the data landscape and the three principal families of data-driven detection and diagnosis methods before articulating several research gaps. Together, these chapters justify the central design choices that follow: anchoring the work on operational electrical and meteorological measurements, building on the a real-world dataset, and pursuing measured embedded validation. Part~II presents the data pipeline, the detection and diagnosis models, and the embedded deployment that operationalise these choices.
