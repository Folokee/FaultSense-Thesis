\section*{Context}

The global energy landscape is undergoing significant changes, driven by the urgent need to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and transition to sustainable energy sources. Solar photovoltaic (PV) technology has become a central component of this transition. By the end of 2025, cumulative installed PV capacity reached approximately 2,383~GW \citep{irena2026}. This growth is supported by reductions in module costs, technological advances, and international commitments to reduce emissions following the Paris Agreement.

PV systems have several characteristics that make them suitable for large-scale deployment. They are modular, operate without emissions, require limited maintenance, and typically have service lifetimes exceeding 25 years. Utility-scale PV installations also benefit from low operation and maintenance costs, averaging around 13~USD/kW/year, compared with 20–93~USD/kW/year for onshore wind \citep{irena_costs2025}.

Algeria has considerable potential for PV development. Sonelgaz currently operates 29 PV plants with a total capacity of 383.1~MW, producing 590.44~GWh in 2024. The government plans to expand capacity by an additional 3,200~MWc through 21 new plants currently under construction \citep{energygov2025}. Ensuring the success of these investments depends not only on installation but also on maintaining reliable operation throughout the systems’ service lifetimes. Effective fault detection is essential to achieve this goal.

\section*{Problem Statement and Motivation}

PV faults are often not visible through direct observation. Unlike conventional generators, where mechanical failures can often be heard or seen, PV anomalies appear primarily as deviations in electrical measurements. Undetected faults can reduce energy yield, accelerate equipment degradation, increase maintenance costs, and in some cases pose safety hazards.

Traditional periodic inspections are adequate for small systems but are impractical for utility-scale PV plants. These installations may contain tens of thousands of modules spread across hundreds of hectares, making frequent manual inspections costly and insufficient to capture faults promptly.

Automated Fault Detection and Diagnosis (FDD) systems address this limitation by continuously monitoring operational data and identifying abnormal behaviour in real time. Machine learning and deep learning approaches have demonstrated strong potential for this purpose. However, two critical limitations remain: most studies rely on simulated datasets rather than real field measurements, and few evaluate model performance under the constraints of embedded edge deployment. This thesis addresses both of these limitations.

\section*{Objectives}

This thesis aims to develop a reproducible and operational FDD framework for PV systems. The work is structured around three objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rigorous data pipeline.} Design and implement a complete workflow for data ingestion, temporal splitting, preprocessing, and feature engineering. The pipeline handles real-world measurement imperfections and prevents data leakage at each stage.
    
    \item \textbf{Lightweight FDD models.} Propose and evaluate efficient machine learning and deep learning models for anomaly detection (detecting whether a fault exists) and fault classification (identifying the type of fault). Models are assessed based on diagnostic accuracy and suitability for embedded deployment.
    
    \item \textbf{Edge deployment.} Deploy the validated solution on a Jetson Nano platform with a real-time monitoring interface, demonstrating practical feasibility beyond laboratory conditions.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this thesis is organised as follows. Part~I presents the theoretical background. Chapter~\ref{ch:pv-systems-fdd} introduces PV fundamentals, fault taxonomy, and relevant AI concepts. Chapter~\ref{ch:related-work} reviews data-driven FDD methods, examines available datasets, and identifies research gaps addressed in this work. Part~II presents the main contributions, including the data pipeline, anomaly detection and fault classification experiments, and edge deployment. The thesis concludes with a summary of findings, discussion of limitations, and recommendations for future research.
